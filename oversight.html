Audits & Oversight

What LAUSD’s own auditors found about technology spending and money paid to private vendors.

LAUSD’s Office of the Inspector General (OIG) is the District’s internal watchdog. It reports to the Board and audits contracts and programs. Every year an outside CPA firm also audits how LAUSD spends voter-approved school bond money. This page pulls together what those reports found about technology spending and contracts with private companies, from July 2024 through June 2026.

Who checks tech spending? Mostly no one.

Almost half of the Inspector General’s $10.6 million budget ($4.5 million) is bond money, which can only pay for oversight of bond-funded projects. Both annual reports say this limits the OIG’s other work, so its audits focus heavily on bond-funded construction. Contracts paid from the general fund get far less scrutiny.

  • The OIG built a data-analytics system covering more than 100 million rows of District financial data. In FY 2025 it could use it only on bond-funded spending; in FY 2026 it used it “mostly” on bond-funded spending, and says it will look at general-fund programs “as funding becomes available.”
  • Audits District management asked for but the OIG couldn’t do for lack of general funds include Procurement of Common Goods at School Sites. Some audits were abandoned midway.
  • Two audit manager and two senior investigator positions were eliminated in FY 2025, and five vacant senior auditor positions were closed.
  • Of the 26 audits the OIG completed in FY 2025, only two examined technology or ed-tech vendors (Mainline Information Systems and HeyTutor). None of the large ed-tech platforms on this site were audited that year.

Vendors that overbilled, underpaid, or didn’t deliver

Rebates vendors owed LAUSD and didn’t pay

Many LAUSD supply and technology contracts require the vendor to pay back a percentage of what the District spends (a “volume rebate”). In a sample of 31 of roughly 362 rebate contracts, 14 vendors (45%) had unpaid or underpaid rebates totaling $615,772, about 30% of what they owed. Another $7 million in purchases on rebate contracts weren’t flagged in LAUSD’s SAP system, so no one was tracking rebates on them. Because only 8.5% of contracts were checked, the OIG says the true shortfall is likely “substantially higher.” LAUSD has no formal process for collecting what vendors owe. (Audit OA 25-1455, June 2025)

Paid for services never performed: solar contractors

An FY 2026 review of LAUSD’s $136.3 million solar program (62 systems at 61 sites) found contractors owed an estimated $616,114 in guaranteed payments for systems that didn’t produce the promised energy, and LAUSD had paid $1,816,174 for operations and maintenance services that were never rendered.

How LAUSD buys IT: no price checks

A June 2025 audit of how LAUSD buys information technology services (OA 24-1452) reviewed three IT contracts. It found the paperwork generally followed District rules, but the basic checks that protect LAUSD from overpaying were missing:

  • No cost analysis. The Procurement Services Division never sent the cost-analysis request its own manual requires for any of the three contracts.
  • No price comparison. For two bidders (AAA Solutions and Responder Systems), Procurement didn’t compare product prices to find the lowest-cost bidder for each product, and the weighted discount figures it relied on were miscalculated.
  • No check that LAUSD got the best deal. Procurement didn’t verify that LAUSD received the best prices or discounts these vendors gave other schools and public agencies.
  • Missing dealer proof. For one bidder, Vector Resources, Inc., the manufacturer’s price list and proof it was an authorized dealer weren’t available. Vector Resources is a partner on two contracts on this site: School Network Upgrades ($82.2M) and School PA & Network Upgrade ($3.5M). The audit doesn’t say which three contracts it reviewed.
  • LAUSD pushed back. It disagreed with the recommendation to create a vendor due-diligence policy and only partly agreed to do cost analyses.

Bond money spent on technology

Voters have approved six LAUSD school bonds since 2002 (Measures K, R, Y, Q, RR and US), totaling $34.2 billion. Bond money can pay for technology, from network cabling and servers to laptops, tablets and software. The FY 2025 bond audit by Simpson & Simpson CPAs (April 2026) shows:

  • IT contractors billed entirely to bonds. Information Technology Services contractors worked on both bond and non-bond projects, but their invoices were charged fully to bond money and not corrected by year-end: $124,999 under Measure Q and $120,737 under Measure RR. LAUSD says it reallocated these in FY 2026. (The RR amount is printed as “$120,737,50” in the report.)
  • A two-year-old IT cost question still unresolved. For the second year, LAUSD couldn’t show how it split the cost of an IT project (the “Soto re-configuration”) between bond money and the general fund. It gave the auditors the same documents as the year before.
  • Software licenses split by estimates. Oracle, Autodesk, Bluebeam, DocuSign and other software was charged to bonds using last year’s numbers or budget estimates instead of actual use.
